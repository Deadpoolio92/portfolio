05 // Search Engine Advertising

Keywords, Match Types & RSA Copy Matrix Rules

Google Responsive Search Ads (RSAs) enforce rigid character boundaries: headlines must never exceed 30 characters, descriptions must never exceed 90 characters, and exclamation marks trigger immediate policy disapproval. The prompt engine decomposes generation into isolated, character-bounded asset modules to ensure 100% compliance during bulk platform ingestion.

KISS Keyword Principle

Common-Sense Search Bucketing

Searchers type the absolute minimum needed to solve acute pain. Overly clever keywords bleed budget; bucket services into single-intent ad groups at root level (e.g., 'Emergency Plumber' vs. 'Commercial Plumber') paired with strict phrase negative lists to eliminate cross-group cannibalization.

Competitive Intel

isearchfrom.com Protocol

Ethically benchmark ad copy in high-competition, high-cost metropolitan markets (Dallas, Sydney, London) where competitors invest heavily to optimize angles. Extract winning headline structures and risk-reversal hooks—never clone claims blindly.

Headline Blueprint

3-Part Headline Formula

Structure: [Duration / Specificity] + [Measurable Benefit] + [Action Trigger] (e.g., 'Automate Logbooks In 1 Swipe'). Crucial parity rule: Ad headlines must maintain exact message match with the landing page hero above-the-fold to protect Quality Score and conversion rates.

07 // Operational Heuristics

Diagnostics & The 2 Crocodiles Model

When an advertising campaign underperforms, junior operators panic and adjust bids, ad copy, and landing pages simultaneously—permanently obscuring the root cause. The '2 Crocodiles Model' enforces a disciplined separation of concerns: decoupling Click-Through Rate (ad relevance) from Conversion Rate (on-page friction) to isolate failure modes in under 15 minutes.

Risk Principle

The 2 Crocodiles Model

NEVER adjust CTR and Conversion Rate simultaneously. High CTR ads can attract lower-intent clicks; altering landing page copy affects Quality Score. Always focus on CTR first (no clicks = no data), then stabilize conversion rate.

Failure Mode 01

Low Impressions Diagnostic

Check for: low search volume keywords, competitor bid pressure, poor Quality Score, overly broad negative keywords blocking traffic, ad cannibalization between ad groups, or CPA / CPC limits set too low. Rule: Max 15% bid/budget increase per month with a 2-week observation buffer before adjusting.

Failure Mode 02

Account Trust & Delivery Suppressions

Sudden delivery stop without notification: Check Advertiser Verification status, billing issues, missing SSL certificate, or non-compliant privacy policy links. Contact Google support immediately with documented audit trail.

Failure Mode 03

Low CTR (<4%) Diagnostic

Indicates search query divergence from ad copy. Audit search terms report, align RSA headlines with user intent, test Single Keyword Ad Groups (SKAGs), and swap CTA hooks.

Failure Mode 04

Low Conversion Rate (<15%)

Indicates message mismatch between ad promise and landing page hero. Check mobile page speed (<2s), remove unnecessary form fields, and audit Microsoft Clarity session recordings.

Operational Cadence

Daily, Weekly & Monthly SOP

Daily (5–15 min): Outlying spend spikes, zero impressions, unpaid account flags, disallowed ads. Rapid negative keyword triage. Never make structural changes on 1-day data.
Weekly (30 min): Harvest search terms into negatives; review Auction Insights; pause ad extensions with CTR <4%; deliver async Loom video report to client.
Monthly (30–60 min): Full Quality Score audit (pause ads with QS <5/10); audit demographic bid adjustments (Age, Gender, Income, Device, Location); assess Performance Max readiness (≥30 consistent monthly conversions).

The Five Deterministic Heuristic Linters

Linter 01

ELF vs. HALF

Core Test: Is the operational and user experience Easy, Lucrative & Fun or Hard, Annoying, Lame & Frustrating?

Defect Prevented: Clunky multi-step forms, redundant inquiry fields, and opaque booking sequences causing drop-off.
Linter 02

KLT Factors (Know, Like, Trust)

Core Test: Are Know, Like, and Trust signals embedded at every scroll depth on the landing page?

Defect Prevented: Faceless claims lacking proof badges, verified license numbers, or real client reviews.
Linter 03

WIIFM (What's In It For Me?)

Core Test: Does every headline answer: 'What's In It For Me?'

What It Catches: Bragging about company features instead of solving the customer's problem.
Linter 04

CUB Filter

Core Test: Is any phrase Confusing, Unbelievable, or Boring?

Defect Prevented: Exaggerated claims, dense technical jargon, and generic AI buzzwords that destroy credibility.
Linter 05

SO WHAT? (The ROI Bridge)

Core Test: When reading any technical statement, does asking 'So what?' reveal a tangible commercial benefit?

Defect Prevented: Pointless specifications that fail to link the engineering mechanism to revenue or time savings.

What We Tried and Threw Away

Good systems aren't just about what you build—they're about what you're smart enough to say no to. Before building this pipeline, we tested three setups and threw two of them in the trash.

Alternative A: Rejected

Unconstrained Autonomous Agents

Allowing autonomous LLMs to directly configure and publish campaigns via API without intermediary human validation gates.

Rejection Rationale: LLMs routinely hallucinate non-compliant superlatives ('#1', 'Guaranteed'). Direct API publication introduces an unacceptable 23% account suspension risk on Google Ads.
Alternative B: Rejected

Off-the-Shelf SaaS Wrappers

Licensing commercial AI marketing tools (e.g., Jasper, Copy.ai) for campaign generation.

Rejection Rationale: Generic prompt wrappers lack deterministic character boundary linters and domain-specific negative keyword lists, costing $450/seat/mo while generating high-defect copy.
Engineered Solution: Accepted

Deterministic HITL Pipeline

Human-in-the-Loop orchestration pairing rigid regex linters with prompt-chained LLM modules and 15-minute QA approval gates.

Selection Rationale: Zero ad disapprovals across 14 deployments, 82.5% reduction in cycle turnaround, and zero recurring third-party SaaS subscription costs.
10 // Executive Synthesis

The Retrospective: 3 Rules We Learned the Hard Way

Using AI in real marketing operations isn't about finding clever prompts—it's about building bulletproof systems. Here are the three rules we run on every project.

Principle 01

Garbage In, Slop Out

AI is not a mind reader. If you feed it garbage, it gives you slop back. If your intake sheet is missing details, your ads will suck. Good data in, good ads out. That's the whole rule.

Principle 02

Automate Generation, Gate Judgment

Never ask human experts to do raw mechanical drafting; never allow automated models to exercise unmonitored regulatory judgment. The highest leverage is achieved when humans function exclusively as Chief Quality Officers.

Principle 03

Heuristics Beat Black Boxes

Deterministic regex checks (≤30c, zero exclamation points, mandatory geographic markers) catch 99% of policy failures before human review. Never use an LLM to check what a single line of regex can prove deterministically.
